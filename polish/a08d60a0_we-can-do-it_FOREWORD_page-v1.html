It is four minutes of one idea sung until it lands: if someone can do it, I can do it. It moves people to tears, to dance, dream, do, and feel inspired. Transformative and influential. That is the whole argument in a chorus.

Underneath it is one ask of any leader, and it is not about past votes. It is about how you decide. What I actually want to understand is a candidate's cognitive tools, the processes they consistently rely on to judge whether information and its source are credible, their readiness to decide, how decisions are in fact made, consistently, and what their protocol does to avoid the natural and tempting rationalization that stands in for rational thinking, which is basically the human condition. Show that map and people can hold you to it, predict you, and trust what comes out.

Two habits follow from it. Keep the law tidy: nothing redundant, every bill logged with its intended purpose and pre-set to expire or to be reviewed against that purpose and the actual impact it had, known and unexpected both. And get the money out: individuals with far too much wealth also have far too much influence over a future they will never live in. Campaign finance and Citizens United are the near fixes. No taxes until ten million, and seriously high taxes above it, is the road. It could scare folks off, but let's be honest: a nation achieving these aspirations would be worthy of any tax rate.

A leader who shows their map, keeps the laws tidy, legible, and communicated to those they affect, and gets the money out would be appreciated. Whether you agree with those priorities or not, the song may still mean something and get you moving. It isn't that specific. It is about mindset more than governance, and mindset, I'd argue, sits under all of it, on all of us to consider. If someone can do it, you can do it.
