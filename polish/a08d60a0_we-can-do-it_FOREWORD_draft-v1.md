This song started as a voice memo. One person noticing something about her own head and saying it out loud: "if someone can do it, I can do it." Not a boast. An observation. The attitude a person has before any argument begins.

It became a campaign song the day someone else's win made the attitude look like a plan.

Here is the argument that sits under the attitude, in the words it was first said in.

On how a person should decide: "figure out what they do know about those things, and then map those things to the ballot, and then tell them how the mapping works and why you mapped them the way that you did, and how they might consider voting if the map holds."

On what to fix first: "If I was to say three things practically, right now, could change and make a big difference, it would be campaign finance, gerrymandering, and Citizens United."

On the ballot: "I don't think parties should be on the ballot at all."

On every rule anyone writes: no new law unless one is removed, and each one carries its intended purpose and a measure of success or a sunset. "The outcome would be stated as goal and a sunset clause with evaluation."

On money: "It's actually dangerous to have people with that much money. They have way too much influence and they're way too far removed from what is experienced by their fellow citizens."

On the builder's habit under all of it: "My preference is always to provide an architecture which makes the fail state impossible."

None of that is in the song. The song is the nerve it takes to say any of it in public and then go do it. If someone can do it, you can too.

Same ticket. Perfect song. You're welcome.
