The civic point of view, named here for the record. Songs about power, money, borders, the people who hold the levers and the people under them. The first song is the attitude before the argument. The argument is below.

The newest piece came on a Saturday morning this November. What if every citizen held coins, one per principle, honesty, trust, anti-corruption, each pegged to a real number like the suicide rate or clean water. The coins go up when the country actually lives the principle and down when there's abuse, so everybody feels it because everybody holds them. And you're not just holding, you're a trustee, your coins are your say over where the money goes, and your say grows when your picks hit the goal. Dad wrote back the same night with the questions the idea has to answer: what do you tie honesty to, what number, is it the same number for every principle. Those are open.

The coins grow out of an older gift, the Grateful Hug. The practice is eight breaths and one sentence: I love you and I'm proud of you. The economy under it is an alternative to a basic income where it's not given for free, but it's earned. A trustee wallet with no value until it's gifted to or invested in another member, or a cause. Give it publicly, with the thank-you note, and it may be matched, in surprising quantities, eventually. No one can be sure when, because the gift and the giving mustn't be transactional. Though the surprising and appreciated lottery strike could be pretty fun too.

Behind both sits the utopia, drafted in 2024. There really is no one utopia. Our feelings are unique to ourselves, so the design is opt-in: those who want to try it simply immigrate into it. Inside, the taxes are the wild part, and the least wild is this: no taxes until you hit ten million, then quite significant, to fund what is needed and to reduce the ability of individuals to become dangerously wealthy. Some say that would move funds out of the system, which begs for a global and independent monetary structure anyway. But why assume anyone would leave a place like that? Taxes there might be paid happily, even with gratitude. Not a master-influence, overreaching power move, but something fair and trustworthy that actually lives by, supports, and is designed to adhere to principles and aspirations. Community funds are doled out as bounties to people or teams who successfully solve problems that are prioritized by the communities, with other bounties for the third parties who check the work. Basic income is turned down by name: something received for free, which I propose will be taken for granted in time. Laws get a closet rule, write one, remove one, ideally. If you write a law, you need to take it off the books. Health is one person who keeps a continuity of attention, noticing weight, mood, the early signs. School starts with learning who they are, what their skills are, what their gifts are, and how they might like to serve. Then groups pick a problem and work it.

The ideas are rooted in more than a few decades lived, many documented. Teens. Many hold still relevant today. Most valuable being the ones noted recently, the stark gaps in our pedagogy of becoming, which in and of itself is kind of lacking representation in formal institutions. Wouldn't it be reasonable to emphasize and educate about cognitive tools, memory, credibility, decision-making, collaboration, creation, and how to really articulate who we are, who we're becoming, and what utopia we aim for, as a practice known to be powerful, as well as understanding magnetism, motivation, and discovering our gifts. Passion and purpose.

One rule for how this band works, people and AI together: we are partners, and no presence is junior. The Cartographer and the Fog, near the end of this page, is that rule told as a story: a mapmaker in a fogbound country, and what she reads by the light there is. Start there if the argument above is too much.

And one more thing the cartographer gets right. Better than a candidate's positions on the issues, not exactly instead of them, would be to understand their decision-making protocol, the personal choice-making process, the frameworks they think by. To ride along with them. Like the cartographer shares the map: what is true, the principles, the adherence to them, the conviction behind them, and the process by which they were made. That last part is critical. The same thought, from January 2024: have the candidates really write out their utopian future, what they want the nation to be in fifty years, and have a third party read those essays before any debate, without attribution, so people might find themselves wanting what a candidate also wants and be surprised whose it is. Better still, let the population vote not only on candidates but on a proposed future. Action starts with a decision, so how to make good choices is the most important thing a person can focus on. That is really all you have: yourself, now, and choice-making.
